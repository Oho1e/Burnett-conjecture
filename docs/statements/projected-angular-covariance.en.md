# Angular covariance and physical measure of the fixed projection

[简体中文](projected-angular-covariance.zh-CN.md)

This delivery concerns lines 1636–1638 and 1703–1709 of the locked manuscript. Let $\Pi$ denote the original fixed infrared-regularized matrix multiplier and $P(\xi)$ its homogeneous principal symbol on ordered matrix components at a nonzero covector.

Consider smooth real matrix fields with common compact support, uniform componentwise $L^2$ bounds, and weak convergence to zero against smooth compactly supported functions. For a fixed real smooth compactly supported spatial test, suppose all ordered component pairings of the original sequence have angular limits given by the covariance $N$. Applying the actual $\Pi$ to the same sequence gives pairings with limiting covariance $PNP^*$. The angular tests are smooth at nonzero frequencies and homogeneous of degree zero. The proof identifies the actual Schwartz projection with its componentwise $L^2$ Fourier multipliers. The fixed low-frequency differences vanish in both slots of these pairings, and the finite sum gives the full matrix limit.

For a supplied amplitude $A$ with image in the original Ricci-symbol kernel, $PAP^*$ vanishes off the null cone at nonzero covectors. On the cone the projected image of symmetric amplitudes has Lorenz polarization. On the same trace $\tau$, the amplitudes $A$ and $PAP^*$ give equal full physical measures when the measurable null shell has an almost-everywhere symmetric background, nonzero covectors, positive semidefinite amplitudes, and image in the symmetric Ricci kernel. The measure restricts $\tau$ to the null shell and weights it by the nonnegative part of one quarter of the real physical contraction. An identity of complex physical contractions holds before the weighting.

Here $N$ is the covariance for the analytical pairings and $A$ the amplitude for physical contraction. Frequency factors carried by derivative pairings factor out of the two-slot projection. Extracting a common witness from the original vacuum sequence, identifying both actual derivative slots, and proving cross-chart compatibility of the same global measure remain to be connected.
