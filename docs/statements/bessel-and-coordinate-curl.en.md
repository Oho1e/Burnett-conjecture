# F05 first-order localization and the original curl

On a Schwartz input, the squared \(L^2\) norm of the Bessel action equals the negative-order Fourier energy of that input. Consider a fixed finite first-order row with smooth, compactly supported real coefficients, allowing a fixed zeroth-order part. The input sequence consists of smooth real components with uniform whole-space \(L^2\) bounds, a common compactly supported cutoff equal to one wherever any component is nonzero, and convergence to zero against every compactly supported smooth real test. If the negative-order Fourier energy of the row applied to this sequence tends to zero, its homogeneous principal angular action tends strongly to zero in \(L^2\).

For the original metric perturbations, the background is smooth and nondegenerate, the sequence is smooth and converges locally uniformly to the background \(g_0\) with respect to the auxiliary metric \(r\), and the intrinsic first derivatives of the perturbations have a uniform local \(L^\infty\) bound in the background geometric volume. In a fixed chart chosen at a point, all first coordinate derivatives of the perturbation components are uniformly bounded on each compact subset of the chart target. For a fixed smooth cutoff \(\chi\) with compact support inside that target and a second compactly supported smooth cutoff \(\theta=1\) on the topological support of \(\chi\), the actual curl principal angular row on \(\chi\partial(g_n-g_0)\) tends strongly to zero in \(L^2\) along the original sequence.

The covariant curl identity with curvature, the compression on the common defect density, and the variable-coefficient Ricci row remain further steps.
